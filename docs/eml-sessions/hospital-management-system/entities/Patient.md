# Patient — dossier

Phase 4, entity 7 of 30. The master record. Everything clinical and everything
billable hangs off one, and §7 of the research says it is kept for decades.

## 3 · Relationships

Seven outgoing, which makes `Patient` the most-referenced entity in the model
after `Encounter`: `Appointment`, `Encounter`, `Admission`, `Prescription`,
`LabOrder`, `ImagingOrder`, `InsurancePolicy`, `Invoice`.

## 4 · Lifecycle

**None.** A patient is registered and then kept. `is_deceased` closes the record
without a process; there is no sequence of states anyone moves it through.

## 5 · Rules

**None on this entity.** The clinical judgements all attach to the thing being
decided — an admission, a prescription, a result — not to the patient.

## 6 · Hooks

```
%%hook beforeCreate stampMedicalRecordNumber on Patient
```

The MRN must be generated by the application and never typed, because a
hand-keyed one is how two patients come to share a number. Its uniqueness is
already enforced by `UK`; the hook is what supplies it.

## 7 · Cross-entity effects

None outward. Inward: almost everything.

## 8 · Access

| Operation | Roles |
|---|---|
| read | `doctor`, `nurse`, `receptionist`, `pharmacist`, `lab_technician`, `radiologist`, `billing_clerk`, `ward_manager`, `hospital_manager`, `administrator` |
| create / update | `receptionist`, `doctor`, `nurse`, `hospital_manager`, `administrator` |
| delete | `administrator` only |

Wide read, and deliberately so: everyone in the list has to identify the patient
in front of them or on their worklist. **The segregation the research asks for
is not achieved by hiding the patient — it is achieved by hiding the clinical
and financial records that hang off them.** A billing clerk reads `Patient` and
cannot read `Diagnosis`; a receptionist reads `Patient` and cannot read
`ClinicalNote`. That is where those restrictions belong and where they are
applied.

`delete` is administrator-only and should in practice never happen — §7 of the
research says records are closed, not deleted.
