# SafeguardingConcern  ·  standalone  ·  category: Student Support

**Role.** A confidential welfare or child-protection concern recorded in factual language, triaged to an accountable owner and retained through referral or closure. It is separate from discipline because the student may be at risk rather than at fault. Whoever reported it does not read it back; they hear the outcome through ConcernFeedback.

**Placement.** Standalone, and deliberately not a kind of `DisciplineIncident`: the student may be at risk rather than at fault, so the record, its readers and its lifecycle differ. The principal works a list of concerns awaiting triage (`principal-untriaged-safeguarding`).

## Fields

| Column | Type | Modifiers | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | string | PK | The record's own identifier. Generated by the application and never keyed; it is never shown as a display value. |
| `reference` | string | UK | The unique case reference used in restricted meetings and external referrals without putting the student's name in an email subject. |
| `student_id` | string | FK | The student whose safety or welfare may be affected. A concern is never filed only against an alleged perpetrator. |
| `reported_by_id` | string | FK | The staff member who received or observed the information and can distinguish direct words from later interpretation. |
| `owner_id` | string | FK OPTIONAL | The designated safeguarding staff member accountable for triage, action, referral and closure. Blank only until triage assigns the case. |
| `occurred_at` | datetime | OPTIONAL | When the event is believed to have happened, if known. It may differ materially from when the school learned of it. |
| `reported_at` | datetime | — | When the concern reached the school. Response-time reporting starts here, so staff record it immediately rather than backdating it later. |
| `category` | string | — | The broad safeguarding theme used for routing and trend review; it is not a finding that an allegation has been proven. |
| `status` | string | — | reported — captured; triaged — risk and owner assigned; investigating — internal facts being gathered; referred — passed to an authorised external body; closed — action and rationale recorded. |
| `risk_level` | string | — | The current operational risk assessment, recorded in plain language because local safeguarding frameworks differ and professional judgement must remain visible. |
| `factual_account` | text | — | What was seen, heard or disclosed, separating direct quotations and observations from assumptions. This is the core evidential record. |
| `immediate_action` | text | OPTIONAL | Steps already taken to protect the student or preserve evidence before formal triage, including who authorised them. |
| `external_reference` | string | OPTIONAL | The case number supplied by an authorised external agency. Blank until a referral is accepted. |
| `closed_on` | date | OPTIONAL | The date the concern was formally closed after actions, referrals and review were recorded. |
| `closure_reason` | text | OPTIONAL | Why no further action remains with the school and where continuing responsibility sits. Required on closure. |

## Cross-entity effects

Tells the reporter what happened to it, without letting them read it: `acknowledgeConcernToReporter` (afterCreate) and `reportOutcomeToReporter` (afterUpdate, when the status changes) write a `ConcernFeedback` row for `reported_by_id` carrying only the case reference, a neutral outcome and a short message. A concern moving to `referred` still has an `external_reference` for the outside agency's number, but nothing notifies that agency. Closing it requires `closed_on` and a `closure_reason` — the two rules that stop a concern disappearing.

## Open questions

Read access is `principal` and `counsellor` only, yet `teacher` may **create**: reporting must be easy and reading must be narrow. The reporting teacher learns the outcome through `ConcernFeedback`. `%%rbac` cannot scope rows, so every `teacher` can read every feedback row; that is why a feedback row carries a case reference and never the student, the account or the risk.
